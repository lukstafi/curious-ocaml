# Curious OCaml editorial review and third edition proposal

The book warrants a third edition built around **representations, interpreters, and laws**. Its distinctive strength is the way concrete OCaml programs expose mathematical structure: type derivatives become contexts, recursive programs become folds, search becomes a choice of interpretation, and continuations connect evaluation with control. Preserve that identity. A targeted structural rewrite, supported by executable examples and fewer unsupported generalizations, would add more value than a large expansion of the topic list.

This review covers the introduction and all twelve current chapter READMEs at the second-edition baseline `a3aac1a`, together with relevant preludes, the build configuration, and the current sensor-fusion executable. It is a full reading and a targeted correctness audit, not a proof of every algorithm, a solution of every exercise, or a complete historical/source-attribution audit. Historical lecture files and alternate chapter drafts were not rewritten. Straightforward corrections have been applied; proposals below remain proposals unless explicitly identified as applied.

## Overall editorial assessment

The opening logic–types–algebra sequence gives the book a recognizable purpose. The progression from direct recursion through accumulators and continuations is particularly useful. The examples involving tree contexts, search, and alternative representations are worth keeping even where their implementations need work.

The current organization makes the reader repeatedly restart. Lambda calculus interrupts the route to modules and useful programs; the very long monad and FRP chapters contain several courses' worth of machinery; the expression-problem chapter repeats evaluator details before drawing out the comparison. Chapter 12 promises a unification but sometimes substitutes categorical terminology for the construction that would justify it.

The prose also repeats assurances that something is beautiful, remarkable, elegant, or powerful before the example has earned that response. Retain the author's curiosity and occasional playfulness, but replace repeated praise and recap with a prediction, a counterexample, or a specific question the next program will answer. The largest editorial opportunity is to shorten transitions and duplicated implementations while giving the actual difficult steps more space.

The three advertised audiences need different reading routes. A mathematically mature novice can follow a derivation yet still need help loading a file or understanding scope. An experienced programmer needs a quick path to pattern matching, modules, and runnable programs. An OCaml user needs the advanced conceptual route. The introduction now gives basic execution conventions and an initial reading route; a third edition should add prerequisite boxes and graded exercises throughout.

## Chapter by chapter direction

| Chapter | Editorial decision | Third edition work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Logic | Keep the mathematical opening, shorten the initial formal burden. | Put a tiny executable program beside each proof rule. Distinguish induction, general recursion, and termination before presenting them as a correspondence. Provide a practical first-session tutorial. |
| 2 Algebra | Keep as a signature chapter. | Separate cardinality, formal power series, and explicit isomorphisms. Introduce an element hole and a subtree hole side by side. Give hints for the more abstract derivative exercises. |
| 3 Computation | Keep and connect forward. | Use one expression language for reduction, interpretation, simplification, and later extension. State when algebraic identities concern mathematical real numbers rather than IEEE floats, overflow, or exceptions. Add an explicit worklist beside CPS. |
| 4 Functions | Keep as an optional deep route. | Separate normal-order calculations from weak call-by-value execution. Label Church and Scott encodings consistently. Replace unsafe toplevel tricks with a small runnable lambda-calculus interpreter where possible. |
| 5 Constraints | Split or sharply refocus. | Type inference and abstract-data-type specifications are two substantial lessons. Make the specification examples explicit about partial operations, and turn their laws into executable tests. Preserve the intentionally flawed association-list implementation as a named counterexample rather than an apparent endorsed map. |
| 6 Folds | Keep folds; reduce application sprawl. | Distinguish shape-preserving `map` from expression rewriting/folding. Keep one search case study in the main path; move the other puzzle and drawing infrastructure to a project. Establish solver completeness before performance comparisons. |
| 7 Laziness | Split streams from numerical analysis. | Give laziness a cost model: demand, sharing, retained history, and cleanup. Make numerical examples finite approximations with a stated domain/error criterion, or give a real exact-rational treatment. A function named `exact` must not stand in for an error proof. |
| 8 Monads | Substantially compress and reorganize. | Begin with one language of search operations and compare interpreters. State additional choice/distribution laws when an algorithm needs them. Move concurrency toward Chapter 9 and large probability examples into a shared project. Keep transformers only after a concrete conflict of effect semantics motivates them. |
| 9 Effects | Keep as a central modern chapter. | Explain continuation ownership, exceptions, cancellation, resource release, and nesting before a larger scheduler. Compare exact enumeration, likelihood weighting, and replay on the same tiny models. Separate a pedagogical handler from a production runtime. |
| 10 Incremental and reactive programming | Rewrite around a shared program. | Define one game state transition and a recorded input trace. Interpret it through streams, incremental signals, and direct-style effects. Show the same expected trace for all three before discussing drawing or performance. |
| 11 Expression problem | Keep the comparison; remove repeated mechanics. | Define the extension requirements first, then compare closed variants, extensible variants, objects/visitors, polymorphic variants, and typed encodings against those requirements. Supply real separately compiled modules for the dynamic-linking example. Consider tagless-final as a focused additional comparison. |
| 12 Categories | Rewrite selectively with stricter mathematical discipline. | Derive a few useful constructions fully: functor laws, fold universality, an adjunction, and a genuinely polymorphic Yoneda example. Keep advanced optics/codensity optional. Every claimed correspondence needs its category, maps, equality notion, and hypotheses. |

## Material still requiring redesign or further verification

These findings are not hidden behind the passing example checks.

**Honey Islands completeness, Chapters 6 and 8.** The board search starts each new island at the first unvisited cell and forces that seed to be kept. The eat/keep branching occurs when exploring its neighbors. A confirmed counterexample uses board size 2 with only `(-4,0)`, `(2,0)`, and `(4,0)` initially containing honey. For one island of size two, removing `(-4,0)` passes `check_correct`, but `find_to_eat` returns no solutions. An isolated first cell cannot be removed by that traversal. The third edition should define soundness and completeness separately and compare every solver against exhaustive subsets on small boards. Check the direct, optimized, monadic, and transformer versions together. This pass has not redesigned those solvers.

**Numerical contracts, Chapter 7.** A small coefficient or three equal rounded partial sums does not certify convergence; `1+x^100` is a useful counterexample. The text now says so, but the algorithms remain heuristic demonstrations. The arbitrary-precision section still computes with floats. Formal-series operations and numerical evaluation need separate contracts; finite/trailing-zero division, convergence radius, cancellation, and underflow need explicit treatment. File-backed streams also need scoped cleanup for abandoned traversal, not only end-of-file behavior.

**Search and effect laws, Chapters 8–9.** A `MONAD_PLUS` signature and a few monoid laws do not guarantee that any implementation can interpret backtracking search equivalently. Left-biased option demonstrates the missing interaction between choice and bind. Several models also assume valid finite weights, inhabited sampling supports, and possible evidence. Replay is now corrected for the documented pure-model setting, but arbitrary side effects, exception-catching models, underflow, and long traces require a more substantial inference design. Fixed-seed tests are evidence against specific regressions, not a proof of statistical consistency.

**Time and resource semantics, Chapters 9–10.** The toy scheduler does not yet manage child failures, cancellation, nested runs, or suspended-thread cleanup. Direct-style scripts need an explicit way to discontinue an abandoned continuation. Reactive events need a logical tick and consumption policy, not only an `option` in a cached graph. The stream game and later game implementations should be made behaviorally equivalent; displaying a paddle is not the same as implementing its collision rule. The GUI examples were not run interactively during this review.

**Binding and parsing, Chapter 11.** Reserved-name preconditions prevent the documented simple name supplies from being mistaken for general capture avoidance. A shared tested substitution implementation or de Bruijn representation would be more convincing than repeating a naming assumption across encodings. The parser material relies on prelude definitions, and its choice explanation now correctly describes enumeration rather than commitment to the first successful parse. Parsers passed to repetition must consume input when they succeed. The separately compiled plugin exercise deserves a complete buildable project and failure tests.

**Exercises and references.** Some tasks need missing rules, probability assumptions, answer criteria, or explicit prerequisites. Label exercises as practice, proof, experiment, or project, and provide selected solutions. Several historical course links, comparative claims, and quoted attributions need a dedicated source audit; this review did not verify every external link or quotation. Remove stale references to an unavailable lecture session, or give a repository path and the required definitions.

## A third edition worth producing

A workable architecture would have four parts, reusing most of the strongest existing material:

1. **Reasoning about programs.** A practical OCaml start; logic and types; algebra and contexts; evaluation, recursion, and cost; modules, invariants, and executable laws. Lambda calculus is a clearly marked optional route.
2. **Representations and interpreters.** Develop one small expression language from syntax through folds, substitution, parsing, typed expressions, and extension. Derive an explicit machine from CPS, connecting continuations with the tree contexts already encountered.
3. **Computation over time and choices.** Streams and demand; search and probability with reference models; monads and effect handlers; ownership and cancellation; a reactive project with a shared transition function and reproducible traces. Add a short, explicit distinction between concurrency and parallel execution.
4. **Mathematical synthesis.** Return to examples the reader already trusts. Explain which transformations preserve structure and why. Establish a few category-theoretic results carefully, then offer optics and more advanced constructions as further study.

Three additions would justify the edition more than broad library coverage:

- **Executable laws and counterexamples throughout.** Derive checks from specifications: map laws, inverse functions, parser consumption, inference against enumeration, scheduler traces, and cleanup behavior. Testing belongs next to the mathematics rather than in a tooling appendix.
- **A representation-to-machine bridge.** Show direct recursion, CPS, defunctionalized continuations, and an explicit evaluator on the same example. This gives the middle and later chapters a common purpose.
- **A complete OCaml 5 effects project.** Own suspended computations, cancel them, release resources, and compare an effect handler with a monadic interpreter using the same tests. This should include limitations and a clear boundary between the book's teaching runtime and application infrastructure.

I would avoid adding another large topic survey. In particular, do not expand category theory before the current correspondences are precise, or introduce a new GUI framework merely to modernize screenshots. Aim to remove duplicated code and rhetorical padding before increasing page count. The edition is worthwhile if a reader can carry one program through several representations and explain what changes, what remains equivalent, and how that claim was checked.

## Verification and publication gates

The targeted mdx suite for Chapters 1–5, 7–9, 11, and 12 passes with OCaml 5.5.1. The sensor-fusion executable builds. Chapter 6 also passes a temporary mdx extraction excluding its single Bogue drawing block. Chapter 10 passes a temporary extraction excluding ten Bogue/Lwd/Incremental-dependent blocks while retaining their plain constants and state type for the subsequent direct-style examples. Those temporary checks do not substitute for the original dependency-complete chapter tests.

The full `dune runtest` command is blocked here by missing `bogue`, `lwd`, and `incremental` packages in the installed switch. A release should run it in a supported dependency-complete environment, exercise the GUI examples, and verify the Lwd event change against actual invalidation behavior. The existing GitHub Pages workflow publishes checked-in output; its green status does not establish that the book's programs or mathematics are correct.

For a third-edition release, require a fresh reproducible build of Markdown, HTML, and PDF; structural and behavioral tests for the principal examples; reference-model checks for every optimized search/inference algorithm; a classification of every skipped block; and a proof/source review of Chapter 12. Visual sampling of generated pages is useful but does not replace a complete pagination and accessibility pass.

## Sources used to check key distinctions

OCaml's [expression reference](https://ocaml.org/manual/5.3/expr.html) and [effect-handler chapter](https://ocaml.org/manual/5.3/effects.html) support the evaluation-order and effect-syntax/continuation distinctions. The [Float reference](https://ocaml.org/manual/4.14/api/Float.html) specifies rounding ties away from zero.

The parametricity qualification follows the distinction developed in [Gibbons and Paterson, *Parametric Datatype-Genericity*](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/jeremy.gibbons/publications/parametric.pdf): polymorphic notation alone is not a license to ignore the language's semantic assumptions. The OCaml comparison counterexample in the correction is directly checkable.

[Abbott, Altenkirch, Ghani, and McBride, *Derivatives of Containers*](https://people.cs.nott.ac.uk/psztxa/publ/tlca03.pdf) supplies the mathematical context for differentiating with respect to an element parameter. The corrected binary-tree equation was also derived directly from `T = 1 + a T²`.

[Riehl, *Category Theory in Context*](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf), especially the chapters on representability/Yoneda and adjunctions, is the reference for separating a concrete encoding from the universal property it is claimed to realize. The proposed book structure and publication priorities are editorial judgments from this review, rather than claims made by those sources.
